3. TIP ANALYSIS - THE COUNTERINTUITIVE WIN ⭐ INTERVIEW GOLD
================================================================================

THE PARADOX:
- Tip PERCENTAGE decreased: 20% → 15%
- But average tip DOLLARS increased: $26.35 → $38.12 (+44.7%)
- Total tips increased: $1,660 → $2,821 (+70%)

WHY THIS MATTERS:
This demonstrates understanding of absolute vs relative metrics. While clients
tipped a lower percentage after I raised rates, the higher base meant I earned
significantly more in absolute dollars. A $200 job with 15% tip ($30) beats a
$100 job with 20% tip ($20).

THE BUSINESS LESSON:
- Premium positioning changes client expectations
- Higher rates don't mean lower tips in absolute terms
- Focus on total earnings, not just percentages
- Data analysis revealed this counterintuitive insight

DETAILED COMPARISON:
                        Early Growth    Strategic Optimization    Change
Average Tip ($)         $26.35         $38.12                    +44.7%
Total Tips ($)          $1,659.77      $2,821.11                 +70.0%
Jobs with Tips          63             74                        +17.5%
Median Tip %            20%            15%                       -5 pts
Mean Tip %              17.3%          17.6%                     +0.3 pts

STORYTELLING TIP:
Start with "Interestingly, my tip percentage actually decreased..." then reveal
the dollar increase. This shows analytical sophistication and business acumen.

12. VOLATILITY ANALYSIS - STRATEGIC TRADEOFF ⭐ THIS IS GOLD
================================================================================

THE PARADOX:
- Early Growth volatility: 68.8% CV
- Strategic Optimization volatility: 72.7% CV
- Volatility INCREASED by 5.6%

WHY THIS IS ACTUALLY GOOD NEWS:

"I traded predictability for profitability. The increased volatility reflects a 
deliberate strategic choice to specialize in premium services rather than maintain 
high volume for consistency."

THE EVIDENCE:
- Average weekly earnings: $535 → $561 (+4.8%)
- Average hourly rate: $56 → $70 (+25%)
- Jobs per week: 3.7 → 2.7 (fewer but higher value)

**I was working LESS but earning MORE.**

THE BUSINESS LESSON:
"This demonstrates that not all stability is desirable. Sometimes volatility 
signals growth, specialization, and strategic positioning rather than chaos. 
The key is whether it's CONTROLLED volatility - which mine was."

The Tradeoff:
"Instead of taking every available job for consistent but lower earnings, I

peak earnings when I worked, but more selective job acceptance."

The Result:
"I chose strategic volatility over stagnant stability - and the 25% increase 
in hourly rate validated that decision. If income stability had been the priority, 
I could have maintained lower volatility by continuing high-volume work. But my
